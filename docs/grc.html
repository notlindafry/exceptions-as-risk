2026 objectives progress

Make control evidence collect itself
Evidence collected automatically is 71% complete — the payments team built a collector for their own controls this quarter, retiring a batch of manual pulls and freeing the audit team for higher-value work.
Controls with an automated test is 55% complete — the first controls that test themselves went live on the platform, catching two setup drifts before they reached a customer.
Take the human out of routine risk intake
New risks arrive as a draft a person just confirms is 60% complete — intake now starts from an AI-drafted record, cutting the time from a risk being spotted to being scored and returning analyst hours to real review.
Findings attach themselves to the right control is 69% complete — findings now link to the control they touch on their own, so none sit unrouted waiting on a person to file them.

Wins we can't measure

The three that mattered most this quarter — chosen for where the business did something, not where we did.

ai-platform

What's deliberately not here. How much risk we're carrying against tolerance lives on the engineering profile — putting it here too would just start an argument about which number is right. There is no single program-health score: a blend of coverage, speed, and reuse would describe nothing. Vendor risk rides inside the coverage and control numbers above where vendors sit in a business unit, rather than getting its own line. How fast we notice a brand-new outside obligation is not tracked — a new one shows up above as a gap, but the speed of spotting it needs a watchlist we do not yet keep.
